The test will cover concepts from Weeks 0 through 7 (and Problem Sets 0 through 7), including Week 7’s lecture on Artificial Intelligence. Among the test’s aims is to assess your newfound comfort with the course’s material and your ability to apply the course’s lessons to familiar and unfamiliar problems.

The test is closed-book: the only resources whom you may turn for help or from whom you may receive help during the test are the course’s heads, emailing the heads@cs50.harvard.edu with questions.

You may utilize during the test one two-sided page of notes (Letter- or A4-sized, typed or written), a pencil or pen, blank paper, and a laptop, nothing else.

In other words, you may not, during the test:

  • browse or search the internet,
  • review any materials other than your one page of notes,
  • use any software (e.g., cs50.dev or VS Code itself),
  • use any artificial intelligence (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, et al. or cs50.ai),
  • provide help to anyone, or
  • receive or solicit help from anyone other than the course’s heads.

Take care to review the course’s policy on academic honesty in its entirety. Note particularly, but not only, that

  • looking at another individual’s work during the test is not reasonable and
  • turning to humans (besides the course’s heads) for help or receiving help from humans (besides the course’s heads) during the test is not reasonable.

Test Format

The test will be adminstered primarily through Canvas, using Proctorio, which is remote-proctoring software that works within your web browser, monitoring your video and screen activity during the test. To use Proctorio, you’ll need to install a browser extension, which is compatible with Brave, Edge, Google Chrome, and Opera, but not Safari. The extension can be uninstalled once the test is complete. You will have exactly three (3) hours to complete the test; you must submit within 3 hours of starting. The test itself is intended to take no more than 3 hours. You may start anytime after the test’s release, and you may submit no later than its deadline:

How to Prepare

Ultimately, how best to prepare depends on how you learn best, but allow us to recommend some of the following resources to help you study.

  1. Review each week’s notes.
  2. Review each week’s source code, if any.
  3. Review each week’s slides.
  4. Review each week’s shorts, if any.
  5. Take past tests and, only after you’ve attempted each of their questions, review their sample solutions. Note that some topics covered in past years might not have been covered in this term. Rely on this year’s lectures and problem sets as the official sources for this year’s topics.
  6. Review problem sets’ specifications, sample solutions, and, if any, distribution code.
  7. Review problem set form questions.
  8. Review each lecture’s video.

How to Take the Test, when the time comes

  1. Open the CS50 Test on Canvas.

  2. Ensure you have the Proctorio Browser Extension installed.
  3. Read the Test instructions thoroughly and ensure you understand all test instructions and course’s honesty policy before proceeding.
  4. Once you begin the test, the 3-hour and 5 minute timer will begin. The test will automatically submit after 3 hours and 5 minutes.

If you run into any trouble with the steps above, email heads@cs50.harvard.edu!
